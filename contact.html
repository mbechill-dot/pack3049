The dens

Who your Scout meets on a Monday

Cub Scouts is run by grade. Your Scout joins the den that matches the grade they are in this year and moves up with the same group of kids every August. Each den has a den leader and an assistant den leader.

Kindergarten

Lions

Their first year. Everything is done shoulder to shoulder with a grown-up, so a parent stays for the meeting.

Den leader position is open. Until it is filled, the Cubmasters run this den. If your Scout is a Lion, this is the job that would help most.

1st grade

Tigers

Still with an adult partner, but starting to run at things on their own. First campout, first derby car.

Den leader and assistant den leader. You will meet both on your Scout's first night.

2nd grade

Wolves

The year it clicks. Knots, hikes, and the first badges they earn without a grown-up hovering.

Den leader and assistant den leader. You will meet both on your Scout's first night.

3rd grade

Bears

Tools, fires, and bigger adventures. This is where Scouts start teaching the younger dens.

Den leader and assistant den leader. You will meet both on your Scout's first night.

4th grade

Webelos

Getting ready for Scouts BSA. More responsibility, more say in what the den does.

Den leader and assistant den leader. You will meet both on your Scout's first night.

5th grade

Arrow of Light

The last Cub year and the highest Cub rank. They cross over to a troop in the spring.

Den leader and assistant den leader. You will meet both on your Scout's first night.

Den leaders are parents. Every one of them started out knowing nothing about Scouting, and the training is free and mostly online. Volunteering starts here.

Wanted: the next Popcorn Kernel, and an assistant

Popcorn pays for everything this pack does, and after the 2026 season both the Kernel and the Assistant Kernel roles open up for 2027 and 2028.

The ask is that you start now, not next August. Shadow this year's sale, see how the order gets built, how the trailer gets unloaded, how storefronts get booked and how the money comes in. Then run it next year already knowing what you are doing, instead of learning it cold in the middle of a season.

Two people, so nobody carries it alone. If you have ever thought you might want a bigger role in this pack, this is the one that matters most. Talk to Michael Bechill or either Co-Cubmaster.

Interested in helping? The pack always has open positions, and most of them take less time than you would think.

Who does what

Every job in the pack, explained

A pack runs entirely on parent volunteers, and most families have no idea what these titles actually mean. Here is the plain version. If any of them sounds like something you could do, that is how most of us started.

Leading the program

The people in front of the Scouts.

Cubmaster

Emcees the monthly pack meetings, supports den leaders, and coordinates the overall program.

Assistant Cubmaster

Helps the Cubmaster run meetings and events, and stands in when needed.

Den Leader

Leads regular meetings for one grade-level den and guides Scouts through their handbook adventures.

Assistant Den Leader

Helps the den leader manage the den and supervise activities.

Committee Chair

Leads the pack committee, recruits qualified volunteers, and handles administration.

Chartered Organization Representative

The direct liaison between the pack and our sponsoring organization, American Legion Post 409. Approves every registered adult leader.

Running the pack

The committee. Mostly behind the scenes, mostly a few hours a month.

Treasurer

Manages the pack budget, accounts, and finances.

Secretary

Takes meeting notes and handles pack correspondence.

Advancement Chair

Tracks Scout achievements and organizes badge and rank awards.

Popcorn Kernel

Coordinates the annual product sale and money-earning projects.

Camp Coordinator

Runs summer camp: sign-ups, forms, packing lists, and the week itself.

Special Event Coordinators

Run a single annual tradition, like the Pinewood Derby or the Blue and Gold banquet. One event, once a year, then you are done.

Every one of these is a parent who said yes once. Training is free, mostly online, and nobody expects you to know anything on day one. Start the adult application or just tell a Cubmaster you are interested.
